# Federal legal-source refresh — 2026-11-11

## Scope

Engineering refresh of the existing federal credit-reporting source set used by CreditRepairAI. Official source pages and the current CFPB/eCFR guidance were checked on 2026-11-11. This is not a legal opinion, attorney approval, formal citator check, or review of state-specific statutes.

## Source verification notes

- 15 U.S.C. § 1681e — official U.S. Code House page now linked through the stable section URL; page text indicated laws through Public Law 119-138 (November 3, 2026). The maximum-possible-accuracy summary remains consistent with the current text.
- 15 U.S.C. § 1681i — official U.S. Code House page indicated laws through Public Law 119-124 (October 15, 2026). The reinvestigation summary remains deliberately high-level; timing and exceptions must be checked against the exact facts and current text.
- 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2 — official U.S. Code House page indicated laws through Public Law 119-120 (October 7, 2026). The source summary retains the distinction between furnisher subsections and does not assert a universal private remedy.
- 15 U.S.C. § 1681c-2 — official U.S. Code House page indicated laws through Public Law 119-138 (November 3, 2026). Identity-theft blocking remains a separate process requiring the statutory documentation; ordinary accuracy disagreements must not be characterized as identity theft.
- 12 C.F.R. § 1022.43 — eCFR page was current as of November 9, 2026; Title 12 showed amendments through November 4, 2026. Direct-dispute coverage, exclusions, destination-address requirements, and furnisher process remain caveated in the source summary.
- CFPB credit-report error guide — page marked last reviewed September 2, 2026; it directs consumers to dispute with the reporting company and furnisher and keep supporting records.
- CFPB accurate-negative-information guide — page marked last reviewed September 2, 2026; accurate negative information generally is not removable merely because it is negative.
- CFPB complaint notice — page marked last modified October 5, 2026; it requires a prior dispute with the CRA and says not to submit while the dispute remains pending or before the stated 45-day threshold.
- FTC CROA enforcement guidance — official FTC page continues to warn against misleading credit-repair claims and advance-fee practices; this 2019 blog is retained as consumer-protection guidance, not as a substitute for current statutory text or legal review.
